Research

My research uses quantitative and spatial modelling to investigate how environmental variation and biotic interactions shape biodiversity and ecological dynamics across spatial and temporal scales. I have worked across different ecological systems, from large-mammal populations and species distributions to tropical forest communities and Arctic organisms.

Across these systems, I am interested in linking ecological patterns to the processes that generate them, using spatial statistical, demographic and process-based modelling approaches.

Biodiversity, Community Assembly & Spatial Ecology

A major part of my research has focused on understanding how spatial structure, environmental heterogeneity and dispersal influence species distributions and community assembly.

During my research at the Helmholtz Centre for Environmental Research (UFZ), I studied plant species–habitat associations, community assembly and spatial point patterns in tropical forest dynamics plots. This work included investigating how topography, environmental conditions, spatial scales and biotic interactions contribute to patterns of tree distributions across life stages.

Environmental Change, Species Distributions & Population Dynamics

My earlier research focused strongly on species distributions, habitat suitability and conservation under environmental change. I worked on the distribution and spatial ecology of Bengal tigers, including habitat suitability, dispersal and landscape-level analyses using field observations and remotely sensed environmental data.

DFG-funded research project

An integrated approach for the conservation of Asian elephant and tiger under climate change: linking remote sensing, species distribution modelling and population models
2016–2019 · MNCN-CSIC, Madrid

As a DFG Postdoctoral Fellow, I developed an integrated modelling approach to investigate the conservation of Asian elephant and tiger under climate change. The project linked remote sensing, species distribution modelling and population models. DFG project record

Plant Demography, Biotic Interactions & Ecological Modelling

My current research is increasingly focused on the demographic processes underlying individual plant performance and population dynamics.

Current postdoctoral research

Plant Demography and Neighbourhood Effects
Postdoctoral Fellow · Department of Ecological Modelling · Helmholtz Centre for Environmental Research–UFZ

As a postdoctoral researcher in the Department of Ecological Modelling at UFZ, I contribute to research developing neighbourhood crowding indices and applying them to multi-plot ForestGEO data to investigate individual plant performance, including growth, survival and establishment.

This work provides an opportunity to connect spatial patterns of neighbourhood interactions with demographic processes and to investigate how these processes vary across forest environments.

Developing research direction

I am interested in extending this demographic perspective toward models that link individual-level processes with population and community dynamics. My developing research direction focuses on quantitative demographic modelling of variation in growth, survival and recruitment across species and environmental conditions.
